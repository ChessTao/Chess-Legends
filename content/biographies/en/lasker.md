# Emanuel Lasker

Emanuel Lasker (1868-1941) was a German chess player, mathematician, and philosopher, the second World Chess Champion (1894-1921).

## Main chess achievements

His results in the World Championship matches are as follows: 12-7 and 12.5-4.5 against William Steinitz in 1894 and 1896 correspondingly; 11.5-3.5 against Frank Marshall in 1907; 10.5-5.5 against Siegbert Tarrasch in 1908; 8-2 against Dawid Janowski in 1909; 5-5 against Carl Schlechter in 1910; 9.5-1.5 against Dawid Janowski in 1910. He lost the title in 1921 to Jose Raul Capablanca with a score of 5-9.

In 1935, at age 66, Lasker finished third in the most robust international tournament in Moscow. In this way, he was an elite player for about five decades, which is an incredible achievement!

## Legacy

Lasker’s reign is the longest (27 years!) of any officially recognized World Chess Champion in history.

Lasker is widely known for his contributions to many openings, such as Ruy Lopez, Queen's Gambit, French Defense, and Sicilian Defense.

Lasker was also a chess author - he published chess magazines most of his life and wrote five chess books. His Manual of Chess can still be found in chess bookstores.

Lasker had earned a Ph.D. in Mathematics with his 100-page 1905 thesis “Zur Theorie der Moduln und Ideale.” This work is regarded as one of the foundations of modern algebra.

Albert Einstein (according to Wikipedia, “one of the greatest and most influential scientists of all time”) was a good friend of Lasker's. In the introduction to Lasker's biography, Einstein wrote: “Emanuel Lasker was undoubtedly one of the most interesting people I came to know in my later life. Few indeed can have combined such a unique independence of personality with so eager an interest in all the great problems of mankind.”
