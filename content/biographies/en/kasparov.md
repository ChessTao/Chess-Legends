# Garry Kasparov

Garry Kasparov (born in 1963) is a Russian and former Soviet chess grandmaster, the thirteenth World Chess Champion (1985-2000).

## Main chess achievements

Kasparov won the Soviet Junior Championship (under 18) in Tbilisi in 1976, scoring 7 out of 9 points at age thirteen (!). He repeated the feat the following year, winning with a score of 8.5 out of 9 (!). He became the youngest master in the world by winning the Sokolsky Memorial in Minsk in 1978, outscoring the norm for 3.5 points (!). One year later, he finished two points ahead of the second player in the international tournament in Banja Luka (Yugoslavia) and got his first rating - 2595 (at the time, number 15 in the world). In 1980, he won the World Junior Chess Championship.

Kasparov won the Interzonal tournament of 1982 to qualify for the Candidates’ matches. One by one, he beat Alexander Beliavsky (quarterfinal, 6-3), Viktor Korchnoi (semifinal, 7-4), and Vasily Smyslov (final, 8.5-4.5) and gained the right to play the World Championship match.

In the World Chess Championship match in 1984, Kasparov was down to 5-0 in a "first to six wins" match. By demonstrating extraordinary fighting skills, Kasparov managed to bring the score to 5-3. Then, the FIDE President stopped the match without a result and announced a new one.

In the second match, Kasparov secured the thirteenth World Chess Champion title by scoring 13-11. After this, Kasparov and Karpov played three more World Championship matches. Kasparov consistently kept his title in all of them by scoring 12.5-11.5, 12-12, and 12.5-11.5.

In 1993, Nigel Short won the Candidates' matches to earn the title match against Kasparov. Neither the champion nor the challenger was comfortable with FIDE, resulting in a split in the championship titles. Kasparov and Short played under the auspices of the Professional Chess Association (PCA), created in 1986 by Kasparov. He won the match with a score of 12.5-7.5. Kasparov defended his title in 1995 in a match against Viswanathan Anand (10.5-7.5).

In 1996 and 1997, he played two matches with standard time control against IBM supercomputers. Kasparov won the first match (4-2). One year later, he lost 2.5-3.5.

The next PCA title match between Kasparov and Vladimir Kramnik occurred in London in 2000. Kramnik won 8.5-6.5 and became the new World Chess Champion (later recognized by FIDE).

When winning the Russian championship in 2004, he commented that it had been the last major title he had never won outright.

After winning the prestigious Linares tournament for the ninth time, Kasparov announced on 10 March 2005 that he would retire from regular competitive chess.

Kasparov played in a total of eight Chess Olympiads. He represented the Soviet Union four times and Russia four times. He won fourteen medals, including team gold medals, all eight times he competed.

Kasparov was ranked world No. 1 for a record 255 months overall for his career, the most in history.

He holds the record for most consecutive professional tournament victories, placing first or equal in 15 tournaments from 1981 to 1990.

In 1999, Kasparov reached an Elo rating of 2851 points. This record stood for over thirteen years.

## Legacy

He was considered by many to be the greatest chess player in history. Kasparov won the Chess Oscar a record eleven times.

Kasparov has written many chess books. Practically all of them became chess bestsellers.

Garry Kasparov is affiliated with one of the first online chess platforms, Kasparov Chess.

He is a Chairman of the Kasparov Chess Foundation, which works with more than 3,500 schools throughout the United States and many more worldwide to promote the study of chess as a cognitive learning tool in curricular classes and after-school programs for elementary, middle, and high schools.
