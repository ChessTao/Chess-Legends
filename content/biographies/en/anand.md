# Viswanathan Anand

Viswanathan Anand (born in 1969) is an Indian grandmaster, the fifteenth World Chess Champion (2007-2013).

## Main chess achievements

In 1985 (at age 15), Anand became the youngest Indian to achieve the title of International Master. At age 16, he became the national chess champion. 1987, he became the first Indian to win the World Junior Chess Championship. One year later, at age 18, he became India's first grandmaster.

In 1993, Anand qualified for his first Candidates’ Tournament, winning his first match but narrowly losing his quarterfinal match to 1990 runner-up Anatoly Karpov.

In the 1995 PCA cycle, Anand won matches against Oleg Romanishin, Michael Adams, and Gata Kamsky and got the right to play in the PCA World Chess Championship match. He lost it against Kasparov with a score of 10.5–7.5

In the 1998 FIDE cycle, FIDE granted Karpov, the reigning champion, direct seeding into the final against the seven-round single-elimination Candidates’ tournament winner. Anand won the tournament, defeating Adams in the final, and immediately faced Karpov for the championship. Despite this disadvantage for Anand, the regular match ended 3–3, leading to a rapid playoff in which Karpov won 2–0. Karpov thus remained the FIDE champion.

Two years later, Anand won the FIDE World Chess Championship 2000 (held in New Delhi, India, and Tehran, Iran).

In 2005, Anand finished in a tie for second place at the 2005 FIDE World Chess Championship, won by Topalov.

In 2007, Anand became the undisputed World Chess Champion, winning the World Chess Championship, an eight-player double round-robin tournament held in Mexico City. Anand convincingly defended the title against Kramnik in the 2008 World Chess Championship match held in Bonn, Germany, with a score of 6.5-4.5. In 2010 and 2012, he defended his title in matches against Veselin Topalov and Boris Gelfand, respectively. In 2013, he lost the title to Magnus Carlsen.

In 2014, Anand won the Candidates’ tournament and challenged the World Champion to a new match. Carlsen won it with a score of 6.5 to 4.5.

Anand won the 2003 and 2017 World Rapid Chess Championships.

Anand is the only player to have won the world chess championship in a tournament, match, knockout format, and rapid time control.

The great Indian won numerous prestigious tournaments and set many records. He is the first player to have won five titles in the Corus chess tournament (Wijk aan Zee, Netherlands). Anand has won the Mainz Chess Classic a record 11 times. He is the first player to have won each of the Big Three super tournaments at the time: Corus (1989, 1998, 2003, 2004, 2006), Linares (1998, 2007, 2008), and Dortmund (1996, 2000, 2004).

Anand has a stellar record at the annual Melody Amber Tournament (two separate Blindfold and Rapid Chess super tournaments played): he has five overall prizes (winning in 1994, 1997, 2003, 2005, and 2006) and has the most "rapid play" titles, winning nine times. He is also the only player to win the blind and rapid sections of the tournament in the same year (in 1997 and 2005).

## Legacy

Anand is considered one of the greatest players in history.

His results made chess one of the most popular sports in India. For Indian chess players, he is an undisputed King of Chess. He got the nickname “The Tiger of Madras”.
