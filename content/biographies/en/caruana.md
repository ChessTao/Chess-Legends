# Fabiano Caruana

Fabiano Caruana (born in 1992) is an Italian and American chess grandmaster, one of the world's strongest players from the early 2010s to the present.

## Main chess achievements

Caruana is a four-time Italian Champion (2007, 2008, 2010, and 2011) and a four-time U.S. Champion (2016, 2022-2024). Caruana won a silver medal in the World Rapid Championships 2014 in Dubai.

He won the Sinquefield Cup 2014 with a performance of 3098 - the highest in history.

Having won the FIDE Grand Prix 2014–15, Caruana qualified for the Candidates’ Tournament 2016, where he was runner-up to Sergey Karjakin. Two years later, he won the Candidates’ Tournament and challenged the reigning World Chess Champion for the match. Caruana lost to Magnus Carlsen in the match, held in New York City in 2018, in the rapid tiebreaks after drawing all twelve classical games.

Caruana shared third-fourth place in the next Candidates’ tournament (in 2020).

By finishing second at the 2021 FIDE Grand Swiss, he qualified for his fourth straight Candidates’ Tournament. Despite a strong start, Caruana eventually finished in fifth place. As one of the main favorites in the 2024 Candidates’ Tournament, Caruana scored half a point less than the winner, Gukesh Dommaraju.

He represented the United States on board one at the 42nd Chess Olympiad, held in Baku in 2016, winning team gold and individual bronze. He led the United States to a silver medal in the Olympiad in Batumi in 2018.

In addition to the above, Caruana has a long list of elite tournament wins: Dortmund (2012, 2014, 2015), Grand Slam Masters Final (2012), Zurich Chess Challenge (2013), Romgaz Kings (2013), Gashimov Memorial (2016), London Chess Classic (2017), Grenke Chess Classic (2018), Altibox Norway (2018), Sinquefield Cup (2018), Tata Steel (2020), Superbet Chess Classic (2023).

## Legacy

According to 2700chess.com on October 8, 2014, Caruana's live rating of 2851 is the third-highest in history (after Magnus Carlsen and Garry Kasparov).

He had a breakout stint as a commentator for the 2021 FIDE World Championship and recently began a popular podcast with his longtime second, Cristian Chirila.
