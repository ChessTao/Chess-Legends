# Levon Aronian

Levon Aronian (born in 1982) is an Armenian-American grandmaster, one of the world's strongest players from the late 1990s until the present time.

## Main chess achievements

In 2001, Aronian placed second in both the World Junior Championship and the Armenian Chess Championship. In 2002, he won both titles. From 2003 to 2005, he strung together three top-four finishes in the annual European Individual Chess Championships.

Aronian won the Chess World Cup 2005 without losing a single game. In 2017, he won the Chess World Cup again and became the only player to win this trophy twice.

In 2006, Aronian won his first elite tournament - Linares. The list of his major wins includes the Tata Steel Chess Tournament in Wijk aan Zee, Netherlands (2007, 2008, 2012, 2014), Tal Memorial in Moscow, Russia (2010, 2011), FIDE Grand Prix stages (Sochi 2008, Nalchik 2009, Palma de Mallorca 2017), Grand Slam Final 2009 and 2014, Alekhine Memorial in Paris and Saint Petersburg 2013, Sinquefield (2015, 2018), Altibox in Norway (2017), Superbet Chess Classic in Bucharest, Romania (2022), WR Chess Masters in Dusseldorf, Germany (2023) and others.

Unfortunately, Aronian was always unsuccessful in the tournaments for the World Championship cycle. In the 2007 World Championship Tournament, he placed seventh out of eight players. Despite being a favorite in the Candidates’ Tournament 2012, he lost to Grischuk in first-round tiebreakers. In the Candidates’ Tournament 2014, Aronian tied 6–7 places (out of 8); two years later, he tied 4–7 places (out of 8); two more years later, he finished the last.

At the same time, Aronian was highly successful in the team competitions. At the 37th Chess Olympiad 2006, held in Turin, the Armenian national team amazingly became the Champions. Two years later, in Dresden, Armenia captured a second consecutive gold medal, led once again by Aronian. In 2012, in Istanbul, Aronian's team seized a third Olympic champion title. Aronian had the best performance rating (2849) of all players on board one. Moreover, in 2011, the Armenian team, led by Levon Aronian, won the World Team Chess Championship in Ningbo, China 2011. This team record remains unprecedented since Armenia has never been ranked in the top 3 by rating.

Aronian is an extremely strong player with short-time controls. He won the World Rapid Chess Championship in 2009. One year later, he won the World Blitz Championship.

Considering his creative style, Aronian was always successful in Fischer Random Chess. In 2003, he earned a match against then-world champion Peter Svidler but lost. Two years later, Aronian won the qualification tournament again, earned a rematch, and won the Chess960 World Champion title. He defended the title in 2007 against Viswanathan Anand and lost it in 2009 to Hikaru Nakamura.

## Legacy

In the first months of 2014, Aronian achieved live (2835) and real (2830) record ratings, making him the fourth-highest-rated player in history. Aronian was no worse than the world's number-three player for nearly four years—from November 2010 to September 2014.
