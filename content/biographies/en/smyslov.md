# Vasily Smyslov

Vasily Smyslov (1921-2010) was a Soviet and Russian grandmaster, the seventh World Chess Champion (1957-1958).

## Main chess achievements

In the 1946 Groningen tournament, Smyslov started a series of high finishes against the strong opposition. As a result, Smyslov was one of the five players selected to compete in the 1948 World Chess Championship tournament to determine the sixth world champion. Smyslov finished second behind Mikhail Botvinnik.

In the 1950 Budapest Candidates’ tournament, Smyslov finished third. After winning the Candidates’ Tournament in Zurich in 1953, Smyslov played a match with Botvinnik for the title the following year. The match ended in a draw after 24 games (seven wins each and ten draws), meaning that Botvinnik retained his title.

Smyslov again won the Candidates’ Tournament in Amsterdam in 1956. His second attempt was successful - he won the match against Mikhail Botvinnik with a score of 12.5–9.5 and became the seventh World Chess Champion. Botvinnik exercised his right to a rematch the following year, winning the title back.

Smyslov did not play another world championship match but continued to play in world championship cycles. In 1983, at 62, he went to the Candidates’ final (the match to determine who plays the champion), losing 8.5–4.5 at Vilnius in 1984 to Garry Kasparov, who was only 21.

Smyslov represented the Soviet Union nine times at Chess Olympiads from 1952 to 1972. His total of 17 Olympiad medals, including eight team and seven individual, is an all-time Olympiad record.

Smyslov also represented the USSR in five European Team Championships and emerged with a perfect medals record: he won five team gold medals and five board gold medals.

In 1991, Smyslov won the inaugural World Senior Chess Championship.

## Legacy

He made enormous contributions to chess opening theory - many variations are named after him.

Smyslov wrote a few chess books about his games and a work about the rook endgames (together with Grigory Levenfish).

Smyslov was a baritone singer and only decided upon a chess career after a failed audition with the Bolshoi Theatre in 1950. He recorded several arias during his life.
