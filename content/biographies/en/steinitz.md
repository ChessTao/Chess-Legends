# William Steinitz

William Steinitz (1836-1900) was an Austrian and, later, an American master, the first World Chess Champion (1886-1894).

## Main chess achievements

He won the title of the first World Chess Champion in the match versus Johannes Zukertort held in New York, Saint Louis, and New Orleans in 1886 (12.5-7.5). Later, he successfully defended his title three times - against Mikhail Chigorin in 1889 (10.5-6.5), against Isidor Gunsberg in 1890 (10.5-8.5), and again versus Mikhail Chigorin in 1892 (12.5-10.5).

Steinitz lost the title to Emanuel Lasker in 1894 (12-7) and lost a rematch in 1896–97 (12.5-4.5).

Steinitz was unbeaten in match play for 32 years, from 1862 to 1894. From 1873-1882, Steinitz went on a historic 25-game winning streak.

## Legacy

In the early 1870s, Steinitz introduced a new style of play and is subsequently regarded as the father of positional chess. He displayed many positional elements of the game that are still recognized today: the importance of a sound pawn structure, space, the bishop pair, creating and utilizing outposts for knights, and the accumulation of small advantages.

Steinitz was an active chess writer. He was the principal chess correspondent of The Field (in London) from 1873 to 1882. In 1885, he founded the International Chess Magazine in New York City and edited it until 1891. In addition to game commentaries of the first World Championship match with Johann Zukertort and the 1889 American Chess Congress, he wrote several articles about Paul Morphy. He wrote the book of the 1889 New York tournament, and also in 1889, he published a textbook, The Modern Chess Instructor, that is still available in the bookshops.
