# Robert James Fischer

Robert James Fischer (1943-2008) was an American chess player, the eleventh World Chess Champion (1972-1975).

## Main chess achievements

Fischer became the youngest US Champion in his first US Championship (1957/1958). Later, Fischer played in seven more US Championships, winning all by at least a one-point margin.

Fischer finished fifth and fourth in his first two Candidates’ Tournaments (in 1958 and 1962, respectively). Fischer scored 8.5 points in the first ten games at the Interzonal tournament in 1967. However, a conflict with the organizers about the schedule led to his withdrawal and elimination from the world championship cycle.

In the USSR vs. Rest of the World match in Belgrade 1970 ("the Match of the Century"), Fischer was the high scorer for his team, with a 3–1 score against Petrosian (two wins and two draws).

Fischer overwhelmed the field of the unofficial 1970 World Championship of Lightning Chess (5-minute games) with 19 points out of 22 games.

Fischer won the Interzonal in 1970 with a 3.5-point margin. In the 1971 Candidates’ matches, Fischer scored an impressive 6-0 against Mark Taimanov in the quarterfinal and Bent Larsen in the semifinal. Then he crushed Tigran Petrosian (6.5-2.5) in the final. These results gave Fischer a run of 20 consecutive wins against the world's top players, a winning streak topped only by Steinitz's 25 straight wins in 1873–1882.

Fischer won the World Championship match in Reykjavik in 1972 and became the eleventh World Chess Champion. In 1975, Fischer refused to play the next title match against Anatoly Karpov (due to a conflict about the match format) and forfeited his title. In 1992, Fischer beat Spassky in a "Revenge Match of the 20th Century" (held in Yugoslavia) with a prize fund of the match of 5 million USD.

Fischer represented the United States on the first board at four Chess Olympiads, winning two silver and one bronze medal.

## Legacy

In 1988, Fischer filed a U.S. patent for a new type of chess clock, giving each player a fixed period at the game's start and adding a small increment after each completed move. Today, all top tournaments and matches use this clock.

On June 19, 1996, in Buenos Aires, Argentina, Fischer announced and advocated a variant of chess called Fischer Random (later also known as Chess960). The goal of Fischer Random was to ensure that a game between two players is a contest between their understanding of chess rather than their abilities to prepare opening strategies or memorize opening lines. In 2019, FIDE organized the first World Fischer Random Chess Championship.

His book My 60 Memorable Games, published in 1969, is regarded as one of the great pieces of chess literature.

Many movies about Fischer’s life and chess career were released, such as Searching for Bobby Fischer, Me and Bobby Fischer, Bobby Fischer Live, and Pawn Sacrifice.

Many consider Fischer to be among the greatest and most famous chess players of all time.
