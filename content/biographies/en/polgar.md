# Judit Polgar

Judit Polgar (born in 1976) is a Hungarian chess grandmaster, the strongest female chess player of all time.

## Main chess achievements

At 9, she won her first international tournament in New York. When Judit was 11, she defeated a Grandmaster in the tournament game. One year later, she became the youngest International Master at the time. In 1988 and 1990, she won the World Junior (for boys!) Championships (U-12 and U-14).

In 1988, at the age of 12, she played with her sisters for Hungary’s women’s team at the Women’s Chess Olympiad, and Judit scored 12.5 points out of 13 on board two, earning individual gold. The national team won gold as well. In 1990, Judit once again led the (same) national team to gold; she also won individual gold. It would be the last women’s-only tournament that she played in.

She entered the top 100 players in the world before turning 13 (!) years old. In 1991, Polgar won the Hungarian National Championship and received the grandmaster title at 15 years and five months. It beat Bobby Fischer’s record by a month.

Between 1990 and 2000, she won or tied for first place at 20 tournaments.

By 2004, Polgar advanced to the 8th place in the world rankings.

In September 2005, Polgar once again made history as she became the first woman to play in the final stages of the World Chess Championship qualification; she had previously participated in large, 100+ player knockout tournaments for the world championship, but this was a small 8-player invitational.

She won a bronze medal at the Men's European Championships in 2011. Polgar represented Hungary eight times as a member of the men's team at the Chess Olympiad, having won two silver medals.

In 2015, Polgar became the coach of the Hungarian men's chess team. Under her leadership, the team won a bronze medal at the European Championships in Iceland.

## Legacy

Polgar became the top-ranked female player in the world in 1989 (at 12 years old), which lasted into her retirement more than 26 years later (a Guinness World Record). Polgar is the best woman ever to play chess.

In 2015, she received the Grand Cross of the Order of Saint Stephen of Hungary, Hungary’s highest decoration and originally an order of knighthood.

Polgar continues to be active in the chess community as a commentator, writer, lecturer, and more. She focuses on her family, the international promotion of the sport, book writing, and the Judit Polgar Chess Foundation, which was established in 2012.
