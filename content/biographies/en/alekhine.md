# Alexander Alekhine

Alexander Alekhine (1892-1946) was a Russian and French chess player, the fourth World Chess Champion (1927-1935, 1937-1946).

## Main chess achievements

In 1914, Alekhine took third place behind Emanuel Lasker and Jose Raul Capablanca in the St. Petersburg 1914 chess tournament. From 1921 to 1927, Alekhine won or shared first prize in about two-thirds of the many tournaments he played. In November 1921, Alekhine challenged the world champion Jose Raul Capablanca for a title match but needed help raising $10,000. To collect money for the game, Alekhine went on tour, playing simultaneous exhibitions for modest fees day after day.

In New York on April 27, 1924, he broke the world record for simultaneous blindfold play when he played twenty-six opponents, winning sixteen games, drawing five, and losing five after twelve hours of play. He broke his world record on February 1, 1925, by playing twenty-eight games blindfold simultaneously in Paris, winning twenty-two, drawing three, and losing three.

In 1927, a group of Argentine businessmen and the president of Argentina guaranteed the funds for the world championship match. Alekhine became the fourth World Chess Champion by beating Capablanca with a score of 18.5-15.5.

Alekhine successfully defended the title in his matches against Efim Bogoljubow: 15.5-9.5 in 1929 and 15.5-10.5 in 1934. But the next title defense could have been better. Alekhine lost to Max Euwe in the Netherlands in 1935, scoring 14.5-15.5. Two years later, these players played a rematch, in which Alekhine took the title back (15.5-9.5).

Alekhine is the only world champion to die while holding the title.

## Legacy

Alekhine is known for his fierce and imaginative attacking style and great positional and endgame skills. To demonstrate his influence on modern chess, Garry Kasparov said: "Alexander Alekhine is the first luminary among the others who are still having the greatest influence on me. I like his universality, his approach to the game, and his chess ideas. I am sure that the future belongs to Alekhine chess."

He is highly regarded as a chess writer and theoretician. Several openings and opening variations are named after Alekhine.
