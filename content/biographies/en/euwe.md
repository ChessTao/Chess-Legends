# Max Euwe

Max Euwe (1901-1981) was a Dutch chess player and mathematician, the fifth World Chess Champion (1935-1937), and FIDE president (1970-1978).

## Main chess achievements

His 12 Dutch chess champion titles are still a record. In 1928, Euwe became the World Amateur Chess Champion.

A few matches were organized in the Netherlands against the strongest players of those years to prepare for the world championship match. Though Euwe’s results in these competitions were not outstanding, he won the title match against Alexander Alekhine in 1935, scoring 15.5-14.5, and became the fifth World Chess Champion. In 1937, he lost a rematch (15.5-9.5).

He participated for the Netherlands in seven Chess Olympiads from 1927-62, always on the first board. Euwe won a total of 102 first prizes in tournaments during his career.

## Legacy

Euwe wrote over 70 chess books; some of the best-known are The Road to Chess Mastery, Judgement and Planning in Chess, The Logical Approach to Chess, and Strategy and Tactics in Chess.

From 1970 until 1978, Euwe was president of FIDE (International Chess Federation). He is considered one of the best presidents FIDE has ever had and the only person in history to hold both World Champion and FIDE President titles.

Max Euwe is a national hero in the Netherlands. In Amsterdam, there is an area known as Max Euweplein, which contains his sculpture.
