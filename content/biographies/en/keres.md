# Paul Keres

Paul Keres (1916-1975) was an Estonian chess grandmaster, one of the world's strongest players between the 1930s and 1960s.

## Main chess achievements

In 1938, he tied for first in the all-star AVRO tournament, held in various cities in the Netherlands and considered one of the strongest tournaments in history. He finished ahead of chess legends Capablanca, Alekhine, Euwe, Botvinnik (four World Champions from different years), Reshevsky, and Flohr. Keres won on a tiebreak by beating Fine 1.5-0.5. The winner of this tournament was expected to be the World Champion Challenger, but the outbreak of the Second World War brought negotiations with Alekhine to an end.

Keres participated in the 1948 World Championship tournament, arranged to determine the world champion after Alekhine died in 1946; he finished joint third. Keres finished second or equal second in four straight Candidates’ tournaments (1953, 1956, 1959, 1962). He was, therefore, occasionally nicknamed "Paul II."

He won the USSR Chess Championship thrice (1947, 1950, and 1951).

Keres represented the Soviet Union in seven consecutive Olympiads, winning seven successive team gold medals, five board gold medals, and one bronze board medal. His four straight board gold medals from 1954 to 1960 is an Olympiad record. Keres also appeared three times for the Soviet Union in the European Team Championships, winning team and individual gold medals on all three occasions.

## Legacy

His death occurred while returning to Estonia from a tournament in Vancouver, which he had won. Over 100,000 were in attendance at his funeral in Tallinn. A statue honoring him is located in Tallinn. A bronze statue of Keres was unveiled on his 100th birthday in his hometown, Narva. There is also a street in Nomme, a district of Tallinn, named after Keres.

The five kroons Estonian banknote bore his portrait.

An annual international chess tournament has been held in Tallinn every other year since 1969. Keres won this tournament in 1971 and 1975. Starting in 1976 after Keres' death, this tournament has been called the Paul Keres Memorial Tournament. There is also the annual Keres Memorial tournament in Vancouver and some chess clubs and festivals named after him.

In 2000, Keres was elected the Estonian Sportsman of the Century.

The World Chess Federation called 2016 "The Year of Paul Keres."
