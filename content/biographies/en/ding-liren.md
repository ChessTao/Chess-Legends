# Ding Liren

Ding Liren (born in 1992) is a Chinese chess grandmaster, the seventeenth World Chess Champion (2023-2024).

## Main chess achievements

Ding won three Chinese national championships (2009, 2011, 2012). He went undefeated (!) in all of those events.

Ding has represented China at all four Chess Olympiads from 2012 to 2018, winning team gold medals in 2014 and 2018 and individual bronze and gold medals in 2014 and 2018, respectively. He also won team individual gold and silver at the World Team Championships 2015.

In July 2016, with a Blitz rating of 2875, he was the highest-rated Blitz player in the world.

In September 2017, he became the first Chinese to qualify for a Candidates’ Tournament. At the Candidates’ Tournament 2018, he placed 4th, the only player without a loss.

In September 2018, Ding became the first Chinese player to pass the 2800 Elo mark on the FIDE world rankings, and in November, he reached a rating of 2816, the joint-tenth highest rating in history.

Ding was undefeated in classical chess from August 2017 to November 2018, recording 29 victories and 71 draws. This 100-game unbeaten streak was the longest in top-level chess history until Carlsen surpassed it in 2019.

In the 2017 and 2019 Chess World Cup, he became the first player in history to reach the finals twice in a row. In the first final, he lost to Levon Aronian; in the second, he lost to Teimour Radjabov.

Ding qualified for the Candidates’ Tournament 2022 by rating. He came in second, qualifying him for the World Chess Championship 2023 against Ian Nepomniachtchi, as Carlsen declined to defend his title. Ding won the 2023 World Championship match to become the first Chinese player ever to hold the title of World Chess Champion. After a hard-fought classical portion that ended tied 7-7 (3 wins for each player), he defeated Ian Nepomniachtchi in rapid tiebreaks, winning in the 4th game (2.5-1.5).

Ding was regarded as an underdog in the 2024 World Championship match against Gukesh Dommaraju, primarily because of his mental struggles throughout the year. Nevertheless, during the match, commentators acknowledged his remarkable fighting spirit. He lost the World Chess Championship title with a score of 6.5 to 7.5 after making one of the most tragic blunders in the championship's history during the final game.
